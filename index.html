Newsletter · 64K List · Beehiiv

Rebuilding a 64K list around behaviour, not batch

Content Marketing & Email Operations Coordinator · Contract, Remote (Austin, TX)

The bottleneck

A 64,000+ contact list was being treated as a single audience. Every subscriber received the same content on the same schedule regardless of how recently they had opened, clicked, or bought — so engaged readers and long-dormant addresses were sent to identically. Open rate had settled at 33.34% and stopped moving.

Tactical solution

  • Segmented by engagement recency — separating recently active subscribers from those who hadn’t opened in months, so send volume matched real interest instead of list size.
  • Split by lifecycle stage rather than signup date, so new subscribers, regulars, and lapsed contacts received appropriately different content.
  • Ran a three-element A/B test every cycle — subject lines, thumbnail and header images, and CTA placement — with variants defined before anything was scheduled.
  • Fed each result into the next send, so the winning variant became the following week’s control rather than a one-off observation.

Campaign Operations · Weekly Cadence

A send calendar that doesn’t slip

Content Marketing & Email Operations Coordinator · Contract, Remote (Austin, TX)

The bottleneck

Shipping four campaigns a week — including a standing Tuesday product newsletter — leaves no room for improvised QA. A broken link, a bad merge tag, or an unsuppressed segment reaches tens of thousands of people at once, and there is no second pass once it’s out.

Tactical solution

  • Segment first — the audience is pulled by behaviour and lifecycle stage before any copy is written, so the message is built for a defined group.
  • Build with variants pre-defined — copy, subject line, and template assembled with the test set decided before scheduling, never bolted on afterwards.
  • Fixed pre-send checklist — links, merge tags, rendering, and suppression rules verified the same way every time. That discipline came directly from raising compliance cases at HSBC and reconciling invoices on SAP at TCS and Genpact.
  • Report and recycle — opens, clicks, and deliverability reviewed after each send and fed straight into the next brief.

Lifecycle Automation · Klaviyo

Automations that run without supervision

Content Marketing & Email Operations Coordinator · Contract, Remote (Austin, TX)

The bottleneck

Campaigns reach whoever is on the list this week. Flows reach people at the moment their behaviour says they’re ready — but only if the trigger and timing are built around that behaviour. Blanket triggers send the right message at the wrong moment, which trains subscribers to ignore the sender.

Tactical solution

  • Welcome sequence built as a staged introduction rather than a single confirmation, so new subscribers arrive with context before the first promotional send.
  • Abandoned-cart flow triggered on cart activity with delay timing tuned to purchase intent, not a fixed interval applied to everyone.
  • Retention sequence targeting subscribers whose engagement had dropped, segmented by how long they had been inactive rather than treating all lapsed contacts the same.
  • Segmentation and suppression rules applied across all three, so a subscriber in one flow isn’t simultaneously receiving a conflicting campaign.
